# The Paper Boat Workshop on Warder Street

An original sample essay for Local Audio Overview. It is fiction, written for this
repository, so the episode has something concrete to say without borrowing a
copyrighted article.

## The room

The Paper Boat Workshop sits at 18 Warder Street, above a shop that sells pencils. It opened in 1998. The door is painted a dull green, and the stairs smell faintly of cedar oil.

## The class

Mina Voss runs the night class. She teaches nine people at a time. Each person folds a skiff from one sheet of 90-gram cotton paper. A finished skiff takes 14 folds. Mina does not allow scissors. She says the edge of the paper is the hull, and a cut edge drinks water.

The class lasts 70 minutes. For the first 20 minutes the room is quiet except for the paper. Then Mina walks the table and reads the folds. She looks for a pinched corner, a crease that missed the center line, and a bow that is tighter than the stern.

## The basin

On the last Thursday of the month the class carries the boats to the stone basin behind the building. The basin is fed by a rain barrel. Boats that last one full minute in the water get a pencil mark on the stern. In April 2024, six of the nine boats earned the mark. The other three took on water at the bow.

## The ledger

Mina keeps a ledger in a gray notebook. The ledger records the date, the paper weight, and whether the boat earned the mark. It does not record names. She says the boat is the student.

## The kit

Visitors sometimes ask for a kit to take home. The workshop sells a kit for 12 dollars. Inside are two sheets of 90-gram cotton paper, a printed crease map, and a note that reads: "Fourteen folds. No scissors. One minute in the water."
